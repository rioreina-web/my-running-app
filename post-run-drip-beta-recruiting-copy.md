# Post Run Drip — Beta Recruiting Copy

Ready-to-send drafts. Personalize the bracketed bits. Keep them plain and human — your audience knows you, so warmth beats polish.

---

## 1. Personal 1:1 invite (your highest-converting message)

> Hey [Name] — I've been building a running app for the last while and I'm finally letting a few people try it.
>
> Quick version: it reads your training the way a coach would. You log your runs (it pulls them automatically) and leave a quick voice note now and then about how things feel, and it tells you what it *notices* — like a fatigue trend, or a pace that's quietly creeping up. It doesn't boss you around or tell you to rest; it just surfaces what's there and leaves the call to you.
>
> I'm only letting ~30 runners in for this first round, and I want people whose honest opinion I trust. You came to mind because [you actually train seriously / you're always thinking about your running / you'd tell me the truth].
>
> Would you be up for trying it for a few weeks and telling me what's good and what's broken? Takes about 5 minutes to set up, then you basically just run. Here's the link: [LINK]
>
> No pressure at all if you're busy — just thought of you. 🏃

## 5. Weekly feedback form questions (keep it to ~6)

Use a free Google Form. Ask the same questions each week so you can see trends.

1. How many runs did you log this week? (0 / 1–2 / 3–4 / 5+)
2. Did you generate a Coach Read? (Yes / No)
3. **Was anything it told you factually wrong?** (Open text)
4. **Did it point out anything you hadn't already realized?** (Yes — what? / No)
5. How useful was it this week? (1–5)
6. One thing you'd change. (Open text)

---

## 6. The 3 tasks every tester gets (so feedback is comparable)

> To make your feedback as useful as possible, here are 3 quick things to try over the next two weeks:
> 1. Log at least 4 runs and 2 voice notes.
> 2. Generate a Coach Read and rate it 1–5 on "accurate" and "useful."
> 3. Tell me one thing it got right and one thing it got wrong.
