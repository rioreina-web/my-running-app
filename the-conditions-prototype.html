How to read this

Every training session is a row, easy runs included. Tap any row and it opens into its splits, each with pace and heart rate.

Key sessions open into a pace sheet. Where you pressed the lap button on a workout, the reps come out as their own table above the splits, with per-rep heart rate. Easy runs have no reps to pull, so they show splits only. That is the whole difference between the two depths.

Download workbook gives you one .xlsx with five tabs — a Read me that explains each one, then Sessions, Splits, Fast segments and Weeks. It exports whatever the filters are showing.

Where you left a voice memo, the session you described sits above the reps the watch recorded — planned against executed, with your own words and tags underneath.

Every row carries the air it was run in: temperature and dew point, the two numbers that decide how hard a given pace feels. Pace runs pale to dark with speed, and each one has a cool-weather equivalent beside it. Mood is the coloured rule down the left edge.
