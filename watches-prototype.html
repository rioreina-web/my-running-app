Watches

A thing to look for in training, written by the athlete or by the coach. The app checks it against real sessions and says something when it's crossed. Six screens: what a person's watches look like, the two ways to make one, the screen that keeps them from being noisy, what firing looks like on a run, and the coach setting one across a roster.

Not shown, on purpose: free-form logic. A watch is a saved row with a metric, a scope, a comparison, a window and a cooldown — the same shape as the five rules already in _shared/rules/, just authored in the app instead of shipped in TypeScript. The language model appears twice: once to turn a typed sentence into that row (screen 3), and once to word the message (screen 5). The check itself is arithmetic.
