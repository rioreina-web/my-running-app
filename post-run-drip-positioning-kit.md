# Post Run Drip — Positioning Kit

**Category:** An intelligent training log. Not an AI coach.

**Purpose:** The name carries the vibe; these lines carry the category. The name never travels alone — everywhere "Post Run Drip" appears, one of these rides with it.

**Revision note (Aug 2026):** This replaces the coach-first framing. `brand-voice.md` positions the product as "a coach that actually knows you" and bans the word AI from all marketing. That rule was written for a prescriptive coach aimed at wary veteran marathoners. The product is now positioned as a training log with an intelligence layer, aimed at data-literate runners who want to understand their own training. See §7 for what changes and what stays.

---

## 1. The hierarchy

One master line, two workhorses. Don't invent a fourth in the moment — pick from these.

| Line | Job | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **A training log you can ask.** | The hook. Category + differentiator in five words. | Landing hero, decks, press, the one you say out loud |
| **Every run, remembered. Every question, answered.** | The expansion. Names memory *and* insight. | Subheads, email, App Store description opener |
| **Half diary, half cockpit.** | Brand flavor. The insider line. | About page, IG captions, merch, plate footers |

The logic: **"training log"** tells a stranger what it is in two words they already understand. **"you can ask"** is the part nobody else has. Every competitor has a log. None of them answer.

## 2. The positioning in one paragraph

> You already log your runs. What you can't do is ask them anything. Post Run Drip keeps the whole record — your paces, your training load, the niggle you had in March, the voice note where you said your calf felt off — and lets you ask it questions. Why is this block harder than the last one? Is my easy pace creeping up? What happened last time I ramped this fast? It answers with your own runs, cites the workouts behind every answer, and tells you when it doesn't have enough to go on.

Use this anywhere a full paragraph fits: press, partner emails, About page, investor summary.

## 3. How AI shows up now

**The old rule:** never say AI. **The new rule:** never say *"AI-powered."* Always say what it actually does.

Generic AI claims read as filler to a data-literate audience. Specific ones read as capability. The test is whether the sentence would still make sense if a competitor pasted it onto their site — if yes, it's too vague to ship.

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| "AI-powered training insights" | "Ask it why last week felt harder. It'll show you the runs." |
| "Smart analysis of your data" | "It reads your last six months and cites the workouts behind every answer." |
| "Personalized recommendations" | "Your pace zones update when you PR, not when a calendar says to." |
| "Advanced machine learning" | "It flags the niggle you mentioned in March when the same pattern shows up." |
| "Get AI feedback on your training" | "Every answer comes with the data. When there isn't enough, it says so." |

Still banned outright, from `brand-voice.md`: crush, journey, unlock, transform, beast mode, grind, wellness, vibes. No emoji. No exclamation points. No "As an AI."

## 6. Landing page hero

**H1:** A training log you can ask.

**Subhead:** Your runs sync automatically. You leave a voice note about how it felt. Then ask it anything — why this block is harder, whether your easy pace is creeping, what happened the last time your calf did this.

**CTA:** Try it for a week.

**Headline image:** a screenshot of a real question and a real answer, citing real workouts. Not a chart, not a hero video, not a runner at sunset. The single most persuasive thing you have is the product answering a question a runner actually asks.

**Section order below the fold:**
1. **The record** — everything in one place: runs, load, notes, niggles, voice memos.
2. **The read** — what it noticed this week, with the workouts behind it.
3. **The ask** — a real Q&A exchange.
4. **The honesty** — "when it doesn't have enough data, it says so." This is the trust close.

## 7. What changed from `brand-voice.md`, and what didn't

**Changed:**
- The hook. "A coach that actually knows you" → "A training log you can ask."
- The AI rule. Total ban → specific claims only (see §3).
- The audience. Veteran self-coached marathoners → data-literate runners who want to understand their training. Younger skew. Optimization-minded.
- The category. AI coach → intelligent training log. Different shelf: Intervals.icu, Runalyze, TrainingPeaks, Strava premium — not Runna.

**Unchanged — these still govern everything:**
- Numbers over adjectives. Grounded, never generic.
- Honest when uncertain. "Not enough data yet" is the trust-builder and the differentiator.
- Peer energy, not authority energy. Runner-to-runner.
- No hype vocabulary, no emoji, no exclamation points.
- Not for beginners. Not a social network. Not a gimmick. Not cheap.

## 8. The two objections to have answers ready for

**"Isn't a training log free?"**
Strava has a log. Your notes app has a log. What none of them have is memory you can interrogate — six months of context that answers a question in a sentence instead of making you scroll. You're not charging for the logging. You're charging for the part that reads it back. Every piece of pricing copy should sit on the ask-and-answer layer, never on the act of logging.

**"How do I know it isn't making things up?"**
Because every answer cites the workout behind it, and when the data is thin it says so instead of guessing. Lead with this — for a data-literate audience, verifiability is the whole product. This is also the single best piece of content you could make: a post showing the app declining to answer.

## 9. One-liners by moment

- **To a runner:** "It's a training log you can ask questions. Six months of your own runs, and it answers with the actual workouts."
- **To the data crowd:** "Everything you log stays queryable. Load, paces, niggles, notes — ask it anything and it answers from your own history."
- **To a non-runner:** "It's a running diary that reads itself back to you and explains what's happening."
- **To a skeptic:** "Every answer cites the runs behind it. When there isn't enough data, it tells you that instead of guessing."
- **TestFlight blurb:** "A training log you can ask. Log runs, leave voice notes, then ask your own training anything."

---

## 10. What not to do

- Never let the name appear cold — no bio, header, or store listing that's just "Post Run Drip" with no category line.
- Never sell the logging. Sell the reading-back.
- Never make a claim a competitor could paste onto their own site unchanged.
- Never say "AI-powered." Say what it does.
- The name is settled. The marketing job is teaching, not renaming.
