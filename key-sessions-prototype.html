1 · The ledger

Trends ▸ Key Sessions. Every quality day is one row: label, date, key pace with the heat-neutral pace under it, a quality-volume bar, and pills for grade, effort HR and conditions. Filter by kind. It is a list, nothing more, and that is the point — it loads fast and reads at arm's length.

The row is the tap target. Everything analytical lives one level down.

2 · The workout detail sheet

What the row opens into, following the existing Plate 23 structure — plate strip, date header, four-stat strip, five-stat secondary strip, chart, splits table, HR zones, weekly context — with the key-session analytics slotted into that skeleton rather than bolted beside it.

  • Four-stat strip becomes key pace · heat-neutral pace · quality volume · back-half fade.
  • The Sharp End is the pace-by-rep chart with HR overlay and the session's own trajectory line.
  • Splits lists the isolated fast reps, not every lap.
  • Vs last session of the same kind is the diff that used to expand inline in the ledger.
  • Conditions, mood, week close it out: heat tax, climb, how it felt, and the ACWR of the week it sat in.

Maps to RunningLog/Workouts/WorkoutDetailPlate23.swift and WorkoutDetailScreen.jsx.

3 · The fitness signal

The trend surface that sits above the ledger on Trends. Raw pace against heat-neutral pace, efficiency in metres per heartbeat, cardiac drift, weekly load with ACWR, and the conditions ribbon that explains the gap between the first two.

The core idea: raw pace is a lie in a Texas summer. adj = (temp°F + dew°F − 100) × 0.1%, capped at 10%, asks every session the same question. Efficiency and drift are the two signals that move before pace does.

Gate it on data_depth 2+ — the trend needs six or more sessions of a kind before it means anything.

The metrics, defined

  • Key pace — average of the fast segments only (laps ≥ 4.6 m/s, ≥ 150 m) for reps; post-warmup average for long and tempo.
  • Heat-neutral pace — key pace with the temp + dew-point penalty removed.
  • Back-half fade — last third of reps minus first third, sec/mi. Positive is slowing. Grades: negative split · held · eased · faded.
  • Drift — for long runs, HR-to-speed decoupling second half against first; for reps, HR rise across the set.
  • Efficiency — metres per heartbeat at quality effort.
  • Quality km — fast or tempo distance only, not session distance.
  • ACWR — this week's km over the trailing four-week average.

Data provenance

21 real key sessions pulled from Strava, 5 May to 25 July 2026: 8 interval sessions from lap data, 1 manual fartlek, 12 long and tempo sessions from time · heart-rate · velocity · altitude streams. Weekly volume from all 100+ runs in the window. HR zones from your Strava zone settings.

Estimated: temperature and dew point come from station climatology for the date and start hour, because the archive weather API is not reachable from this sandbox. Production reads real per-session weather from the existing fetch-workout-weather edge function. Marked EST in the UI.

Sample: the mood pill. Real values come from the voice-log vocabulary.

Notes for the build

The segment engine is a pure function — good candidate for supabase/functions/_shared/ beside pace-heat-adjustment.ts, with the same math mirrored in Swift the way PaceCalculator mirrors workout-helpers.ts.

Pace stays on the blue depth ramp. Coral is reserved for alerts and one punctuation mark per cluster. Green appears only on mood. Empty states use the eyebrow-plus-nudge component, never an em-dash — see the no-heart-rate and nothing-to-compare cases in the sheet.

The Read blocks follow Coach voice: observation, specific numbers, one soft question. No prescriptions, no diagnoses.
