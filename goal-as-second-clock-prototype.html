The goal as a second clock

The app keeps calendar time — today, this week, last 28 days. A goal adds the axis runners actually think in: time to the race. Making the goal central isn't a card or a tab. It's printing that second clock wherever the first one already appears — and saying nothing at all when there's no goal to print.

1 · One sentence, minute one

Onboarding step 3
A goal
What are you
training for?
— in your own words. The app works out the rest and asks you to confirm. —
Run sub 2:20 at CIM

Why a text field beats the chip row. Every real goal on file is already a sentence — "Run sub 2:20 at CIM", "Run a sub 1:10 at Austin Half Marathon", "Run up to 90 mpw". The chips and time picker throw away the race name, and the race name is what lets race-intel find the actual date, course and weather. One field produces a goal the whole system can use; three controls produce one it can't.

The date is not optional. Without it there is no second clock — no countdown, no phase, no week-of-block. Today onboarding invents one 120 days out. A goal with a made-up date is worse than no goal, because everything downstream believes it.

2 · The same clock, everywhere

PlateStrip

One value in one slot, on every plate in the app. It is already built — TrainingDateline — and already documented as this slot's correct value. Three call sites still print a fake figure number instead.

3 · Orientation, never a score

The rule
The goal isThe goal is not
A compass. Which way is the race, and how far.A fuel gauge. No percent-ready, no progress bar.
A time axis. Week 9 of 15 beats "August 24".A deadline that makes a bad week a failure.
A frame. It decides what a number means.A pace source. Fitness sets paces; the goal never does.
Their words. Quoted back, not converted.A grade on whether they're good enough yet.

An athlete can hold a goal they aren't fit for — that is the normal condition of having one. Every element above has to stay true in that case, which is why none of them score anything.
