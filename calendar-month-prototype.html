Prototype · Calendar tab · month view

The month, told as volume, mood, key sessions and niggles

Real Strava data, 1 May – 27 July 2026, 115 runs and 1,135 km, pulled live. Weekly totals reconcile with key-sessions-data.json to within 0.3 km. Mood, journal notes and body mentions are synthetic — those live in Supabase, not Strava — but every one is anchored to something the training actually did on that date. Everything is clickable.

Day bar fill Type — coral key, dark long, light easy. Three values, legible at 17 px.

The one idea

A calendar cell is not a label — it's a bar chart bar. Height is the day's kilometres, on a fixed 0–30 km scale so months compare. That makes every week row a seven-bar chart and the month a stack of five, read left to right like any other chart.

Four channels, no collision

  • Volume — bar height. Doubles split into stacked segments, so a 12 + 6 day looks different from an 18.
  • Session type — bar fill. Coral is a key session, dark warm grey is a long run, light grey is easy. One coral, per the brand rule: intensity is the only thing that gets the accent.
  • Mood — a 3.5 px strip on the bottom edge, full width. Rest days still carry it, which is the point — mood is not a property of a run.
  • Niggles — a bar down the cell's left edge, opacity by severity. Peripheral until you look for it, and unambiguous about which day it belongs to.

Weekly volume is a column, not a footnote

The eighth column is the week. Number, a bar scaled against the block's biggest week, and a mood-average tick underneath. It sits inside the grid so a heavy week and the days that made it are on one line.

What it makes visible

11–17 May. 116 km — the biggest week of the block. The mood strip goes amber then terracotta mid-week and the achilles notch appears. Volume and cost, same row.

25–31 May. The row goes short and gappy. 64 km, finals week. Then 28 May is the fastest long run of the block. Down weeks work.

22–28 June. Three rest cells and a 28.6 km bar in the same row. The hamstring thread explains it.

Interactions

  • Day → sheet with runs, reps, mood, note, niggle mentions. Arrows step day to day.
  • Week column → sheet with the seven bars, ACWR, quality %, mood arc, key sessions.
  • Lens chips → dim everything except key days, long runs, niggle days or hard days.
  • Mood swatches → isolate one mood across the month.
  • Story lines → jump straight to the day or week they describe.
  • Niggle threads → sheet with the mention timeline and the rest days around it.
  • Block spine → 13 weeks of context; tap a bar to change month.

Why the pace spectrum isn't on the day bars

The blue ramp is a within-session encoding — it colours a pace trace, a time-in-zone bar, a rep. It answers “how fast was this bit.” A calendar cell is a whole day: 21 July is a 3.2 k shakeout, a 6×1mi, a 1.6 k jog and a 6.3 k evening. There is no single pace for that day, so colouring the bar by one is an average of things that never happened together.

Flip the toggle above the phone and it shows: ten sequential steps need area, and a 17 px bar doesn't have it. The trends session grid gets away with the spectrum because its dots are sized by effort. Blue is also a fourth hue next to mood green/amber and niggle rose, on warm paper built around one coral.

Where it does belong, and now does: the rep table in the day sheet, and a time-in-zone strip under it. That's the same level the spectrum already lives at in pace-spectrum-mockup.html, and it's the one place in this view with room to read the ramp.

Something the wiring turned up

Binding the spectrum to real data instead of invented thresholds exposed a gap. zones.run_zones_ms (source: RacePace) has five boundaries — 6:46 / 5:50 / 5:14 / 4:54 / 4:36 per mile — so six bands. The published spectrum has ten stops. Four of them can never be reached. Either the zone model gets finer or the spectrum drops to six; right now a legend promises resolution the data can't deliver. Worth deciding before this ships anywhere.

Week view · rows, not columns

The month grid is columns because a cell only has to be a glyph. A week has room, so it flips to seven rows — which is the only shape that fits per-day workout detail, pace mix, sleep, HRV and a niggle word on one line.

This is where the pace spectrum belongs. Each day's bar is width ∝ volume, segmented by pace zone, so 21 July reads as half easy / half MP at a glance and 24 July shows its navy sliver of 200s. Same encoding rolls up into the week's Volume by pace bar.

Sleep and HRV are synthetic

There is no sleep or HRV model in the app yet — VITAL-GARMIN-APPLY-NOTES.md is still un-applied and VitalManager is stubbed. These values are shaped like Vital/Junction's payload (duration_s, efficiency, hrv_rmssd, resting_hr) and driven by what the training actually did: a hard day the night before costs sleep and HRV, and baselines are 28-day trailing means, so a week's delta is measured against the athlete rather than a population.

Patterns state the counter-evidence

Every card opens to the working. Polarisation lists every run with the zone it landed in; load-vs-recovery lists all thirteen weeks so you can see whether this one is unusual; spacing lists every key session with its gap; niggle timing shows the co-occurrence bars and says plainly that categories overlap and none of it is causal. A card that can't show its evidence shouldn't make the claim.

Open questions

Cells are 41 × 62 px — under the 44 px touch minimum on width. Either the row grows to 68 px and the grid scrolls, or the tap target extends into the 2 px gutter. Leaning toward the latter.

Mood is one value per day here. If voice logs produce several, the strip should probably become a gradient rather than average them into mush.

Pattern tone is a three-state dot — green / amber / rose. The thresholds are guesses (78% easy, ACWR 1.08, HRV −4). They want calibrating against more than one athlete before any of them turn into a nudge.
