How the score is built

Each logged day scores its mood label; the number at the top is the mean of the days you logged, ×100. Days with no log are left out, not counted as zero. It compares one block against another — it is not a grade.

The screen

One number, four lanes

  • Score summary at the top — the 30-day average, the change against the previous thirty, and the four counts that made it: logs, miles, low days, niggle days. Low days and niggle days turn coral past five, so a bad block is visible without reading.
  • Four lanes on by default — mood, mileage, niggles, key sessions. TLS and weekly volume are toggles that start off, so the screen opens quiet.
  • One shared date axis. Every lane draws against the same thirty columns, so a bad Tuesday lines up with the Tuesday that caused it. Toggling a lane off shrinks the chart; nothing shifts sideways.
  • The mood lane carries both — day cells for what you actually said, and the 7-day rolling score as the line above them, so the number at the top has a shape.
  • Rolling thirty, stepping thirty. No calendar-month edge cases and no window that varies between 28 and 31 days, which would quietly move the average.
  • Drag across the chart to resolve one day into plain facts — date, mood word, miles, TLS, key session, niggle. Release and it returns to the block summary.
On the score

Where it's weak, so you know

The ladder at the bottom of the screen is the entire definition, on purpose — the number should never be a black box. Two things about it are worth keeping in view:

  • A mean hides clustering. Thirty neutral days and a month of half energized, half injured both average 50. That's why the day cells and the low-day count sit beside the number rather than behind a tap — they are what the average throws away.
  • The labels are ordinal. The gap from energized to positive isn't the same size as tired to struggling, so the weights are a judgment, not a measurement. tired at 0.30 is the one I'd argue with first — a tired day after a hard session is training working.
  • Low days counts struggling and injured only, for that same reason. Tired still pulls the average down, it just isn't counted as a bad day.
Data notes

What's real, what's placeholder

  • Mood uses the shipped closed vocabulary: energized · positive · neutral · tired · struggling · injured, stored as text. The 0–100 is a view-layer derivation.
  • Niggles are body-part mentions from the closed vocabulary — a dot is a mention, not an injury. injured is only ever emitted alongside one.
  • Days, miles, TLS and sessions are seeded synthetic. Volume generates first (a 3-up-1-down build on a rising ramp, with one stretch where the down week gets skipped) and mood generates from it, off acute and chronic strain. So mood tracks load here for a reason rather than sitting beside it as noise — step back a few blocks and the low patches land under the heavy weeks.
