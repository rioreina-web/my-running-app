The thing to know first

Your MP is only 15 s/mi off your HM.

HM reads 5:23, MP reads 5:38. At ±5% each band is ~33 seconds wide, so they overlap by 18 s/mi — every single week in the window, without exception. That's more than half of each band. A mile at 5:30 today is inside both.

Left alone, that double-counts. On 21 Jul all 16.6 of the MP minutes are the same 16.6 minutes already counted as HM. On 23 Jun the entire session — 7.2 min — lands in both. Across the window 47 minutes get claimed twice.

So the chart defaults to split at the midpoint: anything faster than the halfway point between MP and HM counts as HM, anything slower counts as MP. Mutually exclusive, no double-counting, and it matches what a coach means when they say "that was HM effort." Flip the toggle to see the overlapping version.

What the split shows

You've been training two different things.

240 minutes of HM work, 79 minutes of MP work — and they barely occur together. Twelve sessions are HM-only, five are MP-only, and only three contain both. The MP work is nearly all long-run cruising: 16 May, 24 May, 6 Jun and 1 Aug are long runs whose steady miles land in the MP band, not workouts aimed there.

For a half-marathon goal that's a reasonable split. If a marathon enters the picture, the thing this chart makes obvious is that you have almost no deliberate MP work — no session where MP was the target rather than a by-product of running a long run steadily.

Two bands 15 seconds apart don't measure two different systems. They measure one system and an accounting choice.

If you want them further apart

Tighten to ±3%. HM becomes 5:13–5:33, MP becomes 5:28–5:48. Still overlaps, by about 5 s/mi — the anchors are simply too close for symmetric bands to separate cleanly.

Or anchor MP on the goal race instead. The 15-second gap comes from a marathon prediction derived from half-marathon fitness, not from a marathon you've run. If you set a real marathon goal the gap widens and the bands separate on their own.

Or add a heart-rate discriminator. In the overlap strip, your HM-band laps run 165–168 bpm and your MP-band laps run 155–163. That's a cleaner separator than pace is, and it's already in the lap table.
