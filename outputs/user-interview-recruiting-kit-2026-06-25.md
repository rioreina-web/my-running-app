# Maya Interview Kit — Recruit, Schedule, Run, Synthesize

Everything you need to run 5–8 user interviews with self-coached runners.
Designed so a non-technical founder can run it solo in ~2 weeks. Pair it
with the findings in `user-needs-research-2026-06-25.md` and the
`concept-test-one-pager-2026-06-25.md`.

**Goal of these interviews:** confirm or kill the three assumptions that
matter most — (1) runners hunger for *interpretation*, not more data;
(2) they run easy days too hard and underrate rest; (3) "observe, don't
prescribe" relieves rather than frustrates them.

---

## 1. Who you're looking for (the screener)

You want **Maya-shaped** runners. Recruit people who match most of these.
Don't over-filter — 5–8 good conversations beats 30 perfect-on-paper ones.

**Must have:**
- Runs regularly (3+ times/week) and has for 6+ months
- **Self-coached** — no current human/online coach writing their plan
- Has run at least one timed race (5K up to marathon) and remembers the time
- Uses a watch or phone app to track runs (Garmin, Apple Watch, Strava, etc.)

**Nice to have (don't require all):**
- Currently training toward a goal race or time
- Has had a niggle or injury in the last year
- Journals or talks about their running (voice notes, training log, posts)

**Screen OUT:**
- Has a personal coach right now (different product, different needs)
- Brand-new to running (< 6 months) — Maya is not a beginner
- Only does treadmill/gym cardio, doesn't identify as a "runner"

### Screener survey (5 questions, use a free Google Form)
1. How many times per week do you typically run? *(filter: 3+)*
2. Do you currently follow a plan from a personal or online coach?
   *(Yes / No — filter: No)*
3. Have you run a timed race? If so, your most recent distance + time:
   *(free text — must have one)*
4. What do you use to track your runs? *(Garmin / Apple Watch / Strava /
   Coros / other / nothing)*
5. Are you training toward a specific goal right now? *(free text)*

Plus: name, email, time zone, and "Open to a 30-min paid video chat in the
next 2 weeks?"

---

## 2. Where to find them

- **Reddit:** r/running, r/AdvancedRunning (more Maya-like), r/Marathon_Training,
  r/firstmarathon. Post in the weekly community/self-promo thread, or DM
  active commenters. Read each sub's self-promo rules first.
- **Strava:** clubs for your city or goal race; post in a club you're in.
- **Local running clubs / run-crew Instagram or WhatsApp groups.** Highest
  hit-rate because there's existing trust.
- **Your own network, one hop out:** "anyone know a self-coached runner
  training for something?" Friends-of-friends reduce no-shows.
- **Race Facebook groups** (e.g., a specific marathon's training group).

Aim to send ~25–30 invites to land 6–8 scheduled, expecting ~20% no-show.

---

## 3. Recruiting message (copy-paste, adjust per channel)

> **Subject / first line:** Quick favor — 30 min to talk about how you
> train?
>
> Hi — I'm building a tool for self-coached runners and I'd love to learn
> how you actually plan and adjust your training. No pitch, no selling —
> I just want to understand how you do it today and where it's annoying.
>
> 30 minutes on a video call, and I'll send you a **$25 gift card** (or
> a free year when we launch) as a thank-you. I'm talking to a handful of
> runners over the next two weeks.
>
> If you're up for it, grab a time here: [Calendly link] — or reply and
> I'll work around you. Thanks!

Keep it about *them*, not your product. The phrase "no pitch" measurably
raises response rates and keeps the conversation honest.

---

## 4. Incentive

- **$25–40 gift card** per 30-min interview is standard and fair. Amazon/
  Visa eGift is easiest.
- A "free year at launch" works for warm/network contacts but pulls in
  people biased to like you — prefer cash for strangers.
- Send the incentive **within 24h** of the call. It protects your
  reputation for the next round.

---

## 5. Scheduling logistics

- **Booking:** Calendly free tier, or Cal.com. Offer 30-min slots, buffer
  10 min between, cap at 3/day so you stay sharp.
- **Call tool:** Zoom or Google Meet. **Ask permission to record** in the
  booking confirmation and again at the top of the call.
- **Reminders:** auto-reminder 24h + 1h before. No-shows drop a lot with
  the 1h nudge.
- **Confirmation email** should restate: 30 min, video, recorded with
  consent, incentive, and "come as you are — there are no wrong answers."

### Consent blurb (read aloud + include in writing)
> "Thanks for doing this. I'd like to record so I don't have to scribble
> notes — it stays internal to me, just for product research, and I won't
> share it publicly. You can stop the recording or skip any question any
> time. All good?"

## 6. Pre-call checklist (2 min before each)
- [ ] Their screener answers open (race time, goal, tools they use)
- [ ] Recording ready, consent line at hand
- [ ] Note template open (below)
- [ ] Concept one-pager ready to screen-share for the Reaction section
- [ ] Phone on silent, water nearby — you'll talk less than them

---

## 7. Moderator script (30 min)

Rule #1: **talk ~20% of the time.** Ask, then shut up. Silence pulls out
the real answer. Rule #2: never ask "would you use this?" — ask what they
did last time. Past behavior > predicted behavior.

### Warm-up (4 min)
- Tell me about your running right now — what are you training for?
- How long have you been at it?

### Current behavior (10 min) — *no leading questions*
- Walk me through how you decided what to run **this week**. Actual steps.
- When you're deciding, what's open in front of you? What do you look at
  first? *(probe: which numbers do they actually use vs. ignore?)*
- The last time you felt tired or off — what did you do? How'd you decide?
- Tell me about the last niggle or injury you had. What was going on in
  the **weeks before** it? *(listen for unseen load spike)*

### Probe the blind spots (8 min) — *don't lead; see if it comes up on its own*
- How do you know if an easy run was actually easy enough? *(assumption 2)*
- How do you decide when to take a rest day? How does taking one feel?
  *(listen for guilt)*
- When you look at your training data, what do you **wish** it told you
  that it doesn't? *(assumption 1 — the money question; let them ramble)*
- Ever followed a plan that didn't fit your week? What did you do?

### Reaction (6 min) — screen-share the concept one-pager
- React to an app that **observes and asks you questions** instead of
  telling you what workout to do. Gut reaction? *(assumption 3 — watch the
  face, not just the words)*
- React to "your fitness is between a 3:08 and 3:14 marathon, high
  confidence" vs. "projected finish 3:11:30." Which do you trust, and why?
- React to: a voice memo you record after a run that the app reads back to
  you weeks later, next to your numbers.

### Wrap (2 min)
- If a coach friend texted you one thing each week, what would you want it
  to say?
- Anything I didn't ask about that matters to how you train?

> End every call by asking: "Anyone else I should talk to?" — referrals
> are your cheapest recruiting channel for round two.

## 9. Synthesis (after all calls, ~half a day)

1. **Affinity map.** Pull every notable quote onto stickies (FigJam,
   Miro, or literal Post-its). Group into themes. Don't pre-sort — let
   groups form.
2. **Score the three assumptions.** For each, tally how many of the
   8 raised it *unprompted*. 0–2 = weak signal, 3–5 = real, 6–8 = build
   on it.
3. **Watch for the contradiction column.** The interviews that *broke*
   the research brief are the most valuable — they're where the aggregate
   market and your actual users diverge.
4. **One-page readout:** top 3 themes, the assumption scores, 3 killer
   quotes, and "what this changes about the roadmap." Drop it in `outputs/`
   next to this kit.

The decision this should drive: does the **observe-don't-prescribe**
posture relieve or frustrate real Maya-shaped runners? That single answer
reshapes onboarding and the Coach surface more than any feature request.
