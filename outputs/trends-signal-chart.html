HOW TO READ IT

Top — 30-day pace distribution. The x-axis is a continuous pace spectrum, easy on the left, fastest on the right. Each bar's height is how many miles you ran at that pace. Distance runners want a big pale-blue pile on the left (lots of easy aerobic miles) and a small, deliberate tail of faster work — the "polarized" shape. If the middle fills in, you're spending too much time in the grey zone: too hard to recover, too easy to sharpen.

Bottom — the same training over 12 weeks. Each bar is a week's mileage, stacked by pace zone (Easy at the base up to the fast work on top), with workload (ACWR — 7-day load ÷ 28-day average; 1.0 is your normal, the green band is sustainable), mood, and niggle flags underneath.

THE READ

Your distribution is well-shaped — a strong pale-blue base with clean bumps at MP, LT and 5K, and very little grey-zone junk in between. That's the profile of someone training with intent, not just running hard for its own sake.

Over time, the story holds: week 4's volume jump and MP block pushed workload past the line and the calf spoke up, but the reset week pulled it back and the sharpening returned under control.
