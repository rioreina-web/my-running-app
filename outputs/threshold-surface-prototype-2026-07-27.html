What the data forced

Matching on volume alone was not enough

Your July 8 and July 21 sessions are both 6.0 miles of threshold — but July 8 was 10 × 1K and July 21 was 6 × mile. Mile reps are harder to hold pace on. So the prototype matches on volume and rep length, which leaves four structurally identical sessions: Mar 31, Apr 4, Apr 12 and Jul 21, all six mile-reps.

The best chart in here is rep-by-rep heart rate

Same workout in March and July, six mile reps each. Your July heart-rate line sits 4 to 7 beats below the March one on every single rep, in 11 degrees more heat. No regression, no adjustment — just the two lines.

Your own words invert the read twice

The numbers call July 8 your best recent session. Your voice log says "really struggling today." The numbers call July 21 the slow one; your note explains it was a progression that started at marathon pace by design. Neither number is wrong and neither is the whole story.

Mood lives on a different row

The log carrying the laps and the log carrying the voice memo are separate training_logs rows on the same day. Joining on workout_id returns null every time, which is why mood looks missing when it is not. Join on date.

Honest gaps, shown as empty states not zeros

Niggles is effectively empty. Four body_mentions rows total, none on a threshold day, and niggle_resolutions has zero. The row renders an empty state rather than "no niggles," because we don't know that.
