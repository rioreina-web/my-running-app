# Concept Test — One-Pager

A single stimulus you can put in front of runners (in an interview, or
unmoderated via a form) to test whether the core ideas land. Use the
written concept below as-is — screen-share it, or paste it into a survey.
Each micro-concept maps to one assumption from the research brief.

**How to read the results:** you're not looking for "I'd use it." You're
looking for *relief, recognition, and trust* — and for the runner to
restate the value in their own words. If they can't explain back why it's
different, the concept isn't landing yet.

---

## THE CONCEPT (show this exactly)

> **Most running apps hand you a plan and a wall of numbers. This one is
> different. It pays attention to you — what you ran, and how you felt —
> and tells you what it notices, in plain language. It never barks
> workouts at you. It reads your training the way a thoughtful coach
> would, then leaves the call to you.**
>
> You log runs automatically from your watch, and after a run you can
> leave a quick voice note — "legs felt heavy, work was stressful." Over
> time it learns your normal. When something's worth noticing, it says so:
>
> > *"Your last two weeks jumped well above your usual mileage. Your
> > Tuesday 'easy' runs have been at tempo pace. How are the legs
> > feeling?"*
>
> It anchors on your real race history, not a fantasy goal. It gives
> ranges, not fake precision. And when life gets in the way — a cold, a
> missed week, a niggle — it adjusts with you instead of breaking your
> streak.

---

## MICRO-CONCEPT A — Observe, don't prescribe *(tests assumption 3)*

> Imagine the app *never* tells you "do a 6-mile tempo today." Instead it
> shows you what it notices and ends with a question for you to sit with.
> **You** make the call.

**Ask:**
- First gut reaction — does that feel like a relief, or like the app is
  dodging its job? *(this is the make-or-break signal)*
- Would you trust your own call more, less, or the same with that nudge?

**Score:** Relief / Neutral / Frustrated — and capture *why* verbatim.

---

## MICRO-CONCEPT B — Honest ranges, not false precision *(tests "honesty")*

Show both side by side:

> **Version 1:** "Projected marathon finish: 3:11:30"
> **Version 2:** "Your fitness right now is somewhere between a 3:08 and
> 3:14 marathon — high confidence, based on 4 marathon-pace workouts and
> a recent half."

**Ask:**
- Which do you trust more? Why?
- Has a "projected time" from an app ever been wrong for you? What did
  that do to your trust in it?

**Score:** picks V1 / picks V2 / doesn't care — and the reasoning.

---

## MICRO-CONCEPT C — Voice notes read back over time *(tests the qualitative wedge)*

> After a run you leave a 10-second voice note about how you felt. Weeks
> later, the app shows that note *next to the data from that week* — so
> "felt flat and tired" sits beside the mileage spike that probably
> caused it.

**Ask:**
- Would you actually leave that voice note? When — right after the run?
- Seeing your past words next to your numbers — useful, or clutter?

**Score:** would-use / wouldn't / unsure — and the *when/why*.

---

## MICRO-CONCEPT D — Adapts around life *(tests assumption "adaptation")*

> You get sick for a week. Most plans either guilt-trip you or silently
> shuffle everything overnight so you can't plan. This one notices the
> gap, tells you plainly how it's adjusting, and doesn't treat the missed
> week as a failure.

**Ask:**
- Last time life broke your training — what did your app do? What did you
  *wish* it did?
- Does "we noticed, here's the adjustment, no big deal" feel right, or
  too hands-off?

**Score:** resonates / neutral / wants more direction.

---

## Overall reaction (ask at the end)

1. In one sentence, how would you describe this app to a running friend?
   *(If they can't, the positioning isn't clear yet.)*
2. What's the **one** thing here you'd actually want most?
3. What's missing or what worries you?
4. 0–10: how different does this feel from what you use now? *(why that
   number?)*

---

## How to run it

**In an interview (best):** screen-share, read the concept, then walk A→D.
Watch faces on A and B — the words and the face often disagree, and the
face is more honest.

**Unmoderated (faster, more people):** paste the concept + questions into
a Google Form or Typeform, send to your screener list and running
communities. Cheaper, but you lose the "why." Good for a gut-check at
volume *after* a few live interviews tell you what to listen for.

**Reading the scores:**
- **A (observe-not-prescribe)** is the strategic bet. If most runners feel
  *frustrated* rather than *relieved*, you don't kill the idea — you've
  learned onboarding has to teach the value before the product can deliver
  it. That's a critical, cheap thing to learn now.
- **B** near-unanimous for V2 confirms the range+confidence rule is a
  trust feature, not a hedge.
- **C** tells you whether the qualitative/voice wedge is a real behavior
  or a founder's wish. If runners won't leave the note, the fusion idea
  needs a different input.
- **D** confirms (or not) that adaptation is the headline benefit to lead
  with in marketing.

Log results into the same `outputs/` synthesis readout as the interviews
so the numbers and the quotes live together.
