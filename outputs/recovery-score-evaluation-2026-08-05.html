The finding: the bands are mathematically reachable now, but not practically reachable for an athlete who trains ~6 days a week. The "Recent load" factor charges about −3 on any normal training day (0.42 × ~7 mi), so a healthy, well-managed block reads Worn at best and Flat half the time. The scale spans 8–96; your seven months lived in 27–62. A score that calls a strong block "Flat" 54% of the time isn't lying about load — but it is mis-calibrated as a message.

Mood logging collapsed from ~60% of days in Jan–Feb to under 26% since April and 0% so far in August — because mood only reaches the database through voice logs, and the Today screen's mood prompt is local-only (it never writes a row; the comment in TodayPlate18.swift admits it). The 7-day mood window in today's retool rescues a lot — the factor still had something to read on 77.9% of days — but it's reading week-old words. The one-tap check-ins (sleep is built; mood needs the same write-through) are the highest-leverage fix in this whole report.

04 · Does it predict anything yet?

Honestly: not yet — and now that's measured, not assumed. With mood excluded from the score (to avoid grading it on its own input), the score before a bad-mood day averages 46.0 vs 46.2 before an okay day — no separation (n=34, only 18 negative-mood next-days to test against). Correlation with next-day mileage: ρ=0.106 (nothing). The one directionally-right signal: your score averaged 41.7 before rest days vs 45.3 before run days — you already rest when it's low, though that's partly the load math seeing itself. The July knee episode was preceded by scores of 42, 44, 53, 52, 50 — slightly below your mean of 44.9, but one episode proves nothing. And the planned ground-truth column — felt vs planned RPE — is empty in the database (0 rows), so the strongest validation lane can't run at all yet.

The score today is an honest, legible summary of recent training load and whatever words reach it. It is not yet a recovery predictor — not because the design is wrong, but because its two strongest evidenced inputs (self-reported sleep and consistent mood/RPE) aren't flowing yet.

05 · What this evaluation says to do

1 · Recalibrate the bands to lived reality — either shift band edges so a normal healthy week reads Steady (your empirical p25/p50/p75 are roughly 41/45/49 — the current bands put all of that in Flat/Worn), or better, make "Recent load" relative to your own typical daily load instead of a flat 0.42×miles — the personal-baseline move your Daily Read design already specifies. A 7-mile day is normal for you and shouldn't read as fresh fatigue every single day.

2 · Turn on the inputs before tuning the math further — ship the sleep check-in (built today), give the mood prompt the same write-through, and start the RPE extraction. Every validation lane in this report gets stronger with 4–6 weeks of that data.

3 · Re-run this backtest in six weeks — same script, and the felt-RPE lane switches on the moment the column has rows. The retool already earned its keep here: −24% volatility and a mood factor that survives sparse logging. The next win is calibration, not construction.
