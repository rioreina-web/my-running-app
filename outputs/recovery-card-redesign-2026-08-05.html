What changed, and why

1 · The gauge earns its place. The shipped strip is four equal quarters with no marker — it reads as decoration. Proportional widths tell the truth about the scale (Flat spans 8–45, nearly half of it) and the ink marker gives the number a home. The marker goes coral only in Flat — coral is alert, and that's the only alert state.

2 · Bars you can compare across days. The shipped bars scale to each day's biggest factor, so the same −6 draws long on a quiet day and short on a loud one. Fixed ±18 scale (the mood ceiling) makes yesterday's receipt comparable to today's.

3 · Grouped rows. With seven factors the flat list buries the design's own hierarchy. WORDS / RUNS / NIGHTS makes the evidence tiers visible — and quietly explains why an HRV dip can't outshout a logged "tired."

4 · The coverage line. From the Daily Read design §2f: a read from 3 of 7 inputs shouldn't look identical to one from 7 of 7. Confidence changes tone, never the number.
