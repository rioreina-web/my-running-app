# Post Run Drip — The Product Levers That Get You to 20,000 Paid

*What has to be true about the product itself — June 2026*

---

## The core idea

Marketing fills the top of the funnel. **The product decides whether anyone pays and whether they stay.** At 20,000 paid subscribers, the product has to do three jobs, and every feature decision should be judged against them:

1. **Convert** — deliver a value moment fast enough to win the 7-day trial.
2. **Retain** — keep delivering so the subscription survives month 3 (where most apps die).
3. **Spread** — make the value visible and shareable so runners bring other runners.

You already have the raw material for all three. The work is sharpening it. Here's what matters most, roughly in order of leverage.

---

## Lever 1 — Time-to-value: the "aha" must hit on day 1, not week 2

The whole game is getting a runner to the moment where they think *"this noticed something true about my training"* — **before the trial ends.** Most coaching apps can't do this, because they start empty: a new user has no data, so the smart features have nothing to read for two weeks. By then the trial is over and they've churned.

**Your unfair advantage is already in the plan: the 2-year HealthKit back-fill on signup.** This is the single most important product decision you have. It means a runner can open Post Run Drip and *immediately* see a Coach Read built on two years of their real running — races detected, fitness arc plotted, pace zones anchored to their actual PB. The "aha" can happen in the first session instead of week three.

**Make this the centerpiece of the product, not a background nicety:**
- The onboarding's job is to get to the first personalized Coach Read as fast as possible — ideally the first screen after the HealthKit grant is "here's what I already see in your training."
- Auto-detect their races from the back-fill and have them confirm — this is a magical "wait, it knows my marathon?" moment.
- The trial paywall should sit *just after* this moment, when the value is undeniable.

> If you fix one thing on the product side this quarter, make it: **a brand-new user reaches a genuinely insightful, personalized Coach Read within 5 minutes of install.** That number — time-to-first-insight — is your most important conversion metric.

---

## Lever 2 — The moat: voice + data fusion is what they can't get from Strava

Strava shows charts. Runna generates plans. **Neither reads how your training *feels* alongside the numbers.** That fusion — voice logs (mood, fatigue, niggles) read together with volume, pace, ACWR — is the one thing a subscriber genuinely can't get elsewhere. It's both your differentiation *and* your retention hook, because the more a runner journals, the smarter and stickier it gets.

Product implications:
- **Lower the friction on voice logging to near zero.** It's your highest-value data input. The record button being the front door of the Log tab is right — protect that. A 10-second voice note after a run should feel as easy as texting a friend.
- **Visibly pay it back.** When a runner's voice note ("legs felt dead today") shows up *connected* to the data in a Coach Read ("third heavy-legs note this week, against a 30% load jump"), that's the moment they feel seen. Engineer those payoffs explicitly.
- **The niggles surface is underrated as a retention driver.** A body-part timeline that quietly tracks "you've mentioned your left achilles 4 times in 3 weeks" is the kind of thing a runner literally cannot reproduce themselves, and will keep paying to have.

---

## Lever 3 — Honesty as a feature, not a constraint

Your hard rules — predictions ship as *ranges with confidence* never fake-precise single numbers, the AI never diagnoses or prescribes — read like guardrails. **Reframe them as the product's brand.** In a category full of apps that pretend to be your doctor or your coach, an app that says *"3:08–3:14, high confidence, based on 4 marathon-pace workouts and a recent half"* and then leaves the decision to you is genuinely differentiated and genuinely trustworthy.

Trust is what converts a skeptical, experienced runner — your exact audience. Make the honesty *legible*: show the confidence, show what the prediction is based on, let them see the reasoning. "This app doesn't bullshit me" is a reason to pay and a reason to tell a friend.

---

## Lever 4 — Retention loops: tie the subscription to the journey, not the month

Subscription apps die from churn, not from lack of installs. The product has to give a reason to still be here in month 3, 6, 12. Post Run Drip has a structural advantage here: **running is a journey with a built-in time horizon — the race.**

Build retention loops that exploit that:

- **The training-cycle hook.** A runner training for an October marathon should feel they need you *through October.* Anchor the experience on their goal race so the value has a finish line they're pulling toward, not a monthly bill they reconsider.
- **"Here's what changed since you started."** The product gets smarter with use — make that visible. Periodic moments that show the arc ("your easy pace is 12s/mi faster than when you joined") prove the subscription is compounding.
- **Streak-free consistency.** Don't gamify with punishing streaks (off-brand and stressful). Reward *journaling consistency* gently — the more they log, the richer the reads, and the data-depth system already models this. Let depth visibly unlock richer editorial as they engage.
- **Re-engagement that's worth opening.** A weekly Coach Read worth reading is your best retention email. The product *is* the retention mechanism if the read is good.

The metric that matters: **month-3 retention above ~65%.** Below that, fixing the product beats any marketing spend.

---

## Lever 5 — Build virality into the product (running is social)

You will not reach 20k on paid ads alone (the economics don't work — see the growth plan). A chunk of growth has to come from runners bringing runners, and that has to be *built into the product*, not bolted on.

What's inherently shareable in Post Run Drip:
- **The race-anchored fitness card.** A clean, on-brand image — "fitness range heading into race day, anchored on my 3:28 PB" — is the kind of thing runners post. Design shareable artifacts as first-class output.
- **The honest prediction.** "My app thinks I'm in 3:11 shape 👀" is a post. Make the prediction screen one-tap shareable.
- **The PR / milestone moment.** When the back-fill detects a PB or the fitness arc hits a new high, offer a share.
- **A real referral loop.** "Give a month, get a month." Runners recruit training partners constantly. Make referral a visible, rewarded action inside the app, not a buried setting.

Every shareable artifact should carry the Post Run Drip look (warm paper, black ink, one coral accent) so it does brand work even for people who never read the words.

---

## Lever 6 — Expand what they're paying for over time (the five pillars)

The roadmap's five pillars (Training, Understanding, Recovery, Mobility, Strength) aren't just a feature list — they're a **retention and pricing ladder.** v1 sells Training + Understanding. As Recovery (v1.5), then Mobility and Strength arrive, each adds a reason to stay subscribed and, eventually, justification for a higher tier or a premium plan. You don't need them to hit 20k, but sequencing them keeps existing subscribers from feeling the product has stopped growing — which is a quiet churn driver.

Don't build them early. Just know that the path from 5k → 20k is partly *deepening* the product for the people already paying.

---

## What NOT to build (protect focus)

Getting to 20k is as much about saying no. Based on your own decisions:
- **Don't add a free-forever tier yet.** It converts at 2–5% vs. ~40% for a trial-with-card and quadruples the installs you'd need. Keep the trial+paywall model until you have the volume to afford freemium.
- **Don't chase the coach/dyad surfaces.** Maya doesn't use them. Every hour there is an hour not spent on the conversion and retention loops above.
- **Don't rebuild what Strava already wins** (social feed, segments, generated plans). Your wedge is observation, not competition on their turf.
- **Don't ship fake precision or any diagnose/prescribe behavior.** One screenshot of the app telling someone to "rest your IT band" or projecting "3:09:30" undoes the trust that is your entire conversion advantage.

## The product priority stack (if I had to rank it)

1. **Time-to-first-insight under 5 minutes**, powered by the HealthKit back-fill. *(Conversion — highest leverage.)*
2. **The voice + data payoff moment** made vivid and frequent. *(Differentiation + retention.)*
3. **Paywall placed right after the aha**, trial-with-card, annual pushed. *(Conversion.)*
4. **Month-3 retention loops** tied to the race/journey. *(Survival.)*
5. **Shareable artifacts + referral loop** in-product. *(Cheap growth.)*
6. **Honesty made legible** — confidence, reasoning, restraint. *(Trust across all three.)*
7. **Pillar expansion** (Recovery → Mobility → Strength) sequenced for existing subscribers. *(Long-term LTV.)*

## The one-sentence version

You get 20,000 paid users when the product reliably delivers a *true, personal insight in the first five minutes* (back-fill), *keeps getting smarter the more you talk to it* (voice fusion), *never breaks your trust* (honest ranges, no prescriptions), and *gives runners something worth showing other runners* (shareable, race-anchored moments) — and you've ruthlessly cut everything that doesn't serve those four.
