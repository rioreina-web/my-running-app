IDEA 01 strongest signal already computed

True Pace

The pace you'd have run on a normal morning. Ship this as the default line and put the watch pace behind it as a ghost.

— sec/mi  the watch says you got slower
— sec/mi  true pace says you got faster

Your dew point went from 64°F to 72°F across these 12 weeks. Same fitness, heavier air. Untouched, the trend line is a weather chart wearing your name.

How it reads to an athlete. One line, one unit they already know, and a sentence: “On a 64° morning you'd have run this at 5:07/mi.”

Why it's honest. Over the window, watch pace slopes — sec/mi per 4 weeks on interval days — slower. Heat-adjusted slopes — — faster. Same runs, opposite verdict. Only one of them is about you.

What it needs. heatAdjPaceSecMi already exists on every session. The weather in this file is station climatology; wiring fetch-workout-weather per session is the only real work left.

IDEA 02 weather-proof works from run #1

Finish Strength

Second half versus first half, in seconds per mile. Below the line means you finished faster than you started.

— sec/mi  worst fade, first half of the block
— sec/mi  worst fade, second half

This is the one metric heat can't touch — both halves ran in the same weather, on the same legs. Your blow-ups stopped. That's the story of your summer.

Negative split Held Eased Faded

How it reads to an athlete. You already have the vocabulary — NEGATIVE SPLIT / HELD / EASED / FADED. Four words, no coaching degree required. The chart is just those words placed on a zero line.

Why it's honest. Long-run fade slopes — sec/mi per 4 weeks, intervals —. More importantly the range collapsed: your worst long run in the first half of the block gave up +37 sec/mi over the second half; your worst in the second half gave up +19, and the three long runs since then all landed inside ±14.

The honest caveat. The label mix barely moved — 6 of 9 held-or-better early, 6 of 10 late. What improved is the size of the bad days, not their count. Show magnitude, not a “% of good sessions” score, or the chart will claim something the data doesn't.

IDEA 03 fixes a real bug needs the pace ladder

Against Your Ladder

Every rep session scored as seconds faster or slower than the pace that rep length should be run at. One axis, every session type.

Right now a naive “interval pace” line runs from 4:14/mi to 5:28/mi — and none of that is fitness. It's rep length. 8×200 and 6×1mi are not the same question.

Raw rep pace — the broken version

Score each session against the ladder instead, and the noise goes away. Now a dot below the line means you beat what that session asked for.

How it reads to an athlete. “You ran your 1K reps 4 seconds off target, your 200s 11 seconds under.” No comparison across session types is ever implied.

Why it matters architecturally. This is the consumer pace-system-rework.md has been waiting for. derivePaceTableFromGoal() becomes the one place a target comes from, and Trends becomes a fourth caller alongside /pace-chart, the plan builder and subscribe-to-plan. One ladder, finally earning its keep.

IDEA 04 day-one metric zero fitness math

The Rhythm

Did the hard session actually happen this week? Nothing else on this page works for a brand-new beta user. This does, immediately.

— straight weeks with 2+ key sessions

Early May through mid-June you were averaging 1.3 key sessions a week. Since June 15: 2.2. Quality share of your volume went from 6% to 20% — on the same mileage.

Key session Weekly km

How it reads to an athlete. A streak. The single most legible number in fitness apps, and the only one here that means anything in a user's first three weeks.

Why it's honest. Your weekly volume is flat — 64 to 116 km, no trend. What changed is the shape: qualityPct went 6.4% → 19.2% while mileage sat still. A volume chart shows nothing happened. This shows exactly what happened.

Watch out. Streaks punish taper weeks and injury. Cap the message at “rhythm,” never “don't break the chain.”

DON'T SHIP looks great, lies

Meters per heartbeat

The obvious efficiency metric — distance covered per beat. It's already in your data as mPerBeat, and it's a trap in July.

— % change on long runs, May → July

A beta user opens the app after a good block of training and reads “your efficiency is down 1.6%.” They're not less fit. It was 69°F in May and 78°F with a 72° dew point in July, and heat raises heart rate at any given pace.

Long runs Interval days

The tell. Interval days went +1.6% over the same window while long runs went −1.6%. Real aerobic change doesn't split by session type — heat exposure does. Long runs sit in the sun for two hours; 1K reps at 6:30am don't.

If you want it anyway, heat-adjust the pace side before dividing, or ship decoupling (hrDriftPct) instead — it's a within-run ratio, so the weather cancels out the same way fade does.

If you build one

Build Finish Strength. It works on day one, needs no weather service, no pace ladder and no fitness model, it's immune to the single biggest confounder in your data, and it's the only place your numbers currently show unambiguous improvement. True Pace is the one that makes every other chart in the app stop lying — do it second, and the rest inherit it.
