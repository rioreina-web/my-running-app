What came from where

The front door is the canonical screen

Layout, order and proportion are Voice Log · canonical: the mark, the centred lede, the dek, the linked run as a ruled block with its eyebrow, figure, badge and rail, and the 132px button. The mono on the run meta, the date chips and Tap to record is the reference's, kept.

Worth knowing it cuts against your own doc: §1 reserves mono for "transcripts and machine answers, nothing else", and §5 lists these components as still carrying --font-mono labels from the Helvetica era, waiting to be retyped. Either the screen is right or the rule is — they can't both stand.

Source · Voice Log · canonical, v1

The button floats, it isn't padded into place

This is the detail worth stealing from the reference and the thing my last version got wrong. The hero is flex:1 with its contents centred, so the button sits in the middle of whatever room is left after the blocks above it. Nothing is spaced by hand.

It's why the screen looks composed rather than assembled — and it's the one thing that can't be tuned into place with padding, which is what I was doing.

Source · Voice Log · canonical, .hero{flex:1}

Mood is the leading rule again

I'd moved mood to a dot beside the date. That was wrong — CLAUDE.md is explicit that a 2pt coloured rule at a block's leading edge means mood, and nothing else, and 032c is where that rule comes from. It's back, with the mood word repeating it in the same colour at the foot of the entry.

Source · Log Feed · 032c, .entry .in

One meta line instead of three stat tiles

032c puts everything factual on a single tracked line — Aug 20 · 6 × 800m · 6.20 mi — under a short headline. That reads faster than labelled tiles and it's what makes the feed scannable, so the row is now: mood rule, headline, meta line, your words, mood. Five things, four of which are one line each.

Source · Log Feed · 032c, .entry .meta

Voice and typed entries stop looking the same

032c sets every quote in italic mono, which is right for a voice memo — italic mono is the athlete. But a note you typed isn't speech, so it's set in Crimson, the prose role. Scroll the feed and the two separate instantly without a single label doing the work.

The one thing that stayed a label: ▶ Voice in red, because it's a control — it plays the audio.

Source · 032c + system §1, "the one distinction that matters"

The key-session star went blue

032c stars key sessions in red. The locked system gives that job to --session blue — "names a keyed session, and references" — and reserves red for alerts and brand punctuation. A red star also puts two reds in one row next to ▶ Voice, which §5 rules out: one red element per cluster, maximum.

Source · system §2 + §5. Say the word and it goes back to red.

What's here that isn't in either reference

Type a note instead. The app's OR · TYPE NOTES box is a permanently open text field sitting below the fold. It's one line under the button now, opening a composer in place. It's the one thing on this screen that isn't in your reference shot — say the word and it goes, but the typed-note path then needs a door somewhere else.

Search. 032c has chips and no search. At 142 entries it earns its place, so it's one magnifier at the end of the chip row, with the field appearing only when tapped.

And one thing that left: killing the ink rule and the Log run / Check in switch takes the only entrance to check-in mode with it. The mode still exists in VoiceLogView — it just has no door on this screen now. Worth deciding where it goes before this gets built.

The decision this still waits on

colors_and_type.css in your design-system folder is still the old system — warm paper, coral, Crimson and PT Serif — and the iOS app is built on it. Both canonical screens, and this prototype, are the locked one: white stock, #EE2B24, Instrument Sans, Schibsted, Inter.

That swap is about 60 lines in DesignSystem.swift, per your own REDESIGN-SAFELY.md — but it repaints every screen in the app, not just this tab. Worth settling before anyone spends a day on layout.
