Reading it

The correction is worth 3–12 s/mi, and it always helps.

The coral stems only ever point one way — downward, toward faster. Biggest single correction is 28 Apr: 5:39 raw becomes 5:27 adjusted on a 74° dew point, 12 seconds. On 7 Apr, a 46° dew point, there's no stem at all because there was nothing to correct.

Two sessions stand out, and the HR lane says which.

28 Jul is the best session in the window: 6.5 mi in band at 5:16 adjusted, 165 bpm, on a 73° dew point. Compare 4 Apr — 6.0 mi at 5:14 adjusted but 168 bpm on a cooler day. Nearly identical pace and volume, three fewer beats, far worse conditions, four months apart.

One thing to decide

The +10% slow edge is letting long runs in.

Four sessions here are long runs, not workouts — 16 May, 24 May, 6 Jun and 1 Aug. Their steady miles land at 5:51–6:04 adjusted, which sits inside the slow half of a band whose edge is 5:55. So 4.0 of the 24 minutes credited on 1 Aug is long-run cruising, not threshold.

Two ways to handle it, both cheap: tighten the slow edge to +7% (band becomes 5:07–5:46), or keep +10% and require the lap to also clear a heart-rate floor — say 160 bpm, which every genuine threshold session here clears and the 1 Aug long run does not. The HR floor is the better one, because it survives a hot day where pace alone wouldn't.
