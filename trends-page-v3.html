One rhythm, six times

The old tab gave every section a different shape — some had an eyebrow and a headline and a lede and a read, some had accordions, some had a drill-down arrow. Four different reading patterns on one screen is what made it feel out of control.

Now every section is identical: number, name, one headline figure, chart, one line. Once you've read section 01 you know how to read 06.

The headline figure carries the section

Top-right of each header: 55 mpw, 61% easy, 20 sessions, 16 logs. That's the five-second version. The chart is for when you want more, and the single line under it is the only prose on the screen.

Comparison is a tool, not a screen

Pick any two sessions from the dropdowns and the table redraws — output on top, cost below, the stronger number in black. It defaults to your two most recent interval sessions. It lives inside Key sessions rather than being its own destination.

Deleted

  • EFFORT / FITNESS / SIGNAL segmented control — sections replace it
  • TRENDS 2 — nothing left in it that this screen doesn't hold
  • DATA CHECK · LOOKS OFF — a maintenance chore, belongs on the workout in Log
  • Accordions — mood and niggles are sections now, not drawers
  • Zone-chip pace trends (Mile / 5K / 10K / HMP / MP) — four points a line, axis labels colliding, and a flat result read as “nothing happened”
  • “The work behind it” stacked quality-minutes — five stacked blues no one can read at phone width
  • “Same pace, cheaper” HR line — five points, 158–172 bpm, noise presented as trend

The overlap

Volume, key sessions, mood and niggles now share one 12-week axis in section 01, so a week reads straight down. Columns with no day off are tinted — that tint is what makes the correlation visible rather than something you have to be told.

Drag across it. On 13 July the readout gives you: 59.2 mi, 7 runs, no day off, 2 key sessions, mood positive/struggling, knee sore. Six facts, one week, one line.

Niggles gets a track rather than a section — there are only three mentions in the whole database, so a section would be empty 10 weeks out of 12. As a track it costs one row and pays off exactly when it fires.

Two data corrections

Duplicates. Jul 1, Jul 18 and Jul 21 were each logged twice — identical distance and duration, one row with a vital_workout_id and one without. Worth checking LogDedup.swift.

My source was incomplete. The earlier numbers came from pace_segments, which only 177 of 255 logs carry, so runs were silently missing. Rebuilt on the full deduplicated set, several figures moved: 716 mi not 656, 7 days off not 11, 7 of 12 flat weeks not 5, and load balance is inside the band every week, not 11 of 12.

The correction strengthens the finding rather than weakening it: the seven flattest weeks and the seven weeks with no day off are now the same seven weeks, exactly, and both knee mentions sit inside them.

Provenance

20 key sessions with lap and heart-rate detail from Strava, and 84 consecutive days of mile splits from training_logs.pace_segments, 4 May – 26 July 2026. Temperature and dew point are climatology estimates in this sandbox; production reads real per-session weather from fetch-workout-weather.
