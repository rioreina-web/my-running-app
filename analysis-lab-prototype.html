Signal 01 · HR drift over long runs

Drift is real, and it rides the dew point.

Decoupling: how much more your heart cost per unit of speed in the second half of a long run than the first. Under 5% is aerobically steady. Above it, the engine was working harder to hold the same output — fatigue, heat, or both.

Steady 0–5% In the band Over 5% Fitted trend

Long runs and the 20K tempo, 4 May – 26 July. Drift = second-half HR-to-speed ratio against first-half, moving samples only, gradient held under ±4%.

The two red sessions are the two biggest days — the 2-hour run (27 Jun, +7.3%) and the 18K in 70° dew (6 Jun, +6.2%). Every negative-split long run sits at or below zero. The trend line creeps up as summer arrives — which is why signal 05 exists.

Signal 03 · HR efficiency

Metres per heartbeat is flat. In this heat, flat is a win.

Distance bought per beat at quality effort. It should fall as dew point climbs — the heart spends beats on cooling. It hasn’t. Reps and long runs are two different instruments, so they get two lines, one axis.

Rep sessions Long & tempo Fitted trend per series

19 key sessions with heart-rate streams. The fartlek (30 Jun) is manual entry — no HR, so no dot; the gap is drawn, not smoothed over.

Rep efficiency 1.78 → 1.77 m/beat May to July; long runs 1.54 → 1.54. Holding efficiency while dew point climbed eight degrees is the same finding as signal 05 wearing different clothes: the engine is fine, the weather is expensive.

Signal 04 · Mood × volume × training load

Mood doesn’t follow the load score.

Three lanes, one 12-week axis. The load score is an individual number per week: every mile counts once, every quality mile counts three times — sum, not average, so a big week can’t hide behind an easy one. Mood stays a word, never a number; it gets a lane, not a score.

Load score Miles Quality miles Mood lane Niggle

Drag across a week to read all three lanes at once. Load score = miles + 2 × quality miles.

The three struggling weeks average a load of 78; the four positive weeks average 81 — and the single biggest score of the block (96, week of 29 Jun) was a positive week. Whatever mood is tracking, it isn’t the score. It tracks the calendar: both knee weeks and two of three struggling weeks sit inside the no-day-off streaks from the Trends page.

Signal 05 · Heat impact

Your summer wasn’t a fitness decline.

The dew-point correction, applied to all 20 key sessions. Top chart: what the heat charged you, per session — raw pace in coral, the same run heat-neutral in navy. Bottom: the charge against the dew point it happened in.

Raw · what the watch said Heat-neutral · same run
One session Fitted trend

Penalty = raw minus heat-neutral, sec/mi. Weather is climatology-estimated in this sandbox; production reads per-session values from fetch-workout-weather.

Raw, July’s intervals read 11 s/mi slower than May’s. Heat-neutral, they’re faster — 4:46 best in July against 4:57 in May. The season charged 15–20 s/mi and the correction hands it back; without it every summer chart lies to you.
