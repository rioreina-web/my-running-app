What the data says
The middle line drifts a second a mile faster per month. The shaded range is where the truth could sit — it still includes “no change”, so the card won’t claim one. The band narrows every time you log a session.

Why this works. A bare line through seven noisy points looks like a finding. The same line with its band drawn looks like what it is. And it improves itself: nothing in the code changes, the band just tightens until the answer is real.

What the data says
Your heart rate fell about 1 beat a month while the dew point climbed 4°F. Some of that easing is fitness and some is your body adapting to the heat — this chart doesn’t pretend to separate them.

Why this works. It answers the question by showing the ingredients instead of a score. Crucially it makes the heat confound visible rather than burying it inside an index — which is the failure that sank the first card.

What I got wrong, and what actually changed

I was wrong to say “don’t ship a trend.” The problem was never the line. It was a line with no band, drawn under a headline that claimed more than seven sessions can support.

The heat model is the strong part of your stack. Fit on your long runs it explains 96% of the variance — 0.18% of pace per degree of dew point. That means heat-neutral pace is a trustworthy input; Option 1 leans on it and never touches heart rate, so the acclimation confound can’t reach it.

Sample size is the only thing standing in the way, and it fixes itself. Seven quality sessions give a band of roughly ±8 s/mi. Around twenty-five brings it near ±3, which is a real answer. You are three months of training from this card being worth reading — and it gets there on its own.
