Same session both times: 10 × 1K, reps 5:10–5:22, avg 5:18, 1:05 float. The question is only how the marks carry it.

Why the bars are short today. One lap in this session — a 0.01 mi auto-lap that reads 3:28/mi — sets the axis floor. It renders 2 px wide, so you can't see it, but it drags the scale to 3:00–6:00. Your reps live in an 11-second band inside a 180-second axis — 6% of the range — and bar height is measured up from the slowest gridline. Three quarters of the plot is dead space, and the difference between your best and worst rep is about 2 px.

Scale to the work reps only — by lap role, not a pace ratio, so a glitch lap can never own the axis. Axis tightens to 5:05–5:25 and the spread finally shows. But now the bars over-correct: rep 2 is 3.5% faster than rep 9 and reads three times taller. That's the classic truncated-baseline exaggeration — the axis is honest, the bar length still isn't.

Baseline moves to the rep average. Bars rise when faster, fall when slower. Length now means seconds off target — a real quantity with a real zero. The fade across reps 8–10 is the first thing you see instead of something you have to squint for.

Drop bars entirely. A dot per rep on a tight pace axis, joined by a thin line, rest shown as the gap between them. No zero-baseline promise to break — and it keeps absolute pace on the y-axis where a bar chart can't.

My pick: Deviation, with the Honest-axis fix underneath it. The axis guard is worth shipping either way — it's the bug. The deviation baseline is what turns a hero chart into one that actually earns the space. Dot ladder is the right call if this chart ever moves out of hero position.
