Don't borrow
their floor.
Build one.

Strava's axis floor is arbitrary — whatever makes the bars fit. You have something they don't: the athlete's own zone table. Put the floor on a zone anchor and bar height stops being decoration. It becomes how deep into the work you were.

What the chart says now. Ten reps, all sitting in the band between LT and 10K pace, two of them breaking into 10K, one slipping back toward LT at the end. That's a coaching sentence you get for free from the axis — and Strava structurally cannot print it, because their y-axis is just numbers.
Zone ladderThe one

The y-axis is a zone ladder, not a number ladder. Bands are the athlete's own paces; the floor sits one anchor below the slowest rep. Bars are tall because the work genuinely was — a 5:18 rep really is most of the way from HMP to 5K, and the picture now says so. Width is distance, gap is float, ink depth is pace.

The rule, so it never breaks again
  1. Work reps only. Filter by lap role, never by a pace ratio — that's what let a 0.01 mi auto-lap own the axis.
  2. Ceiling = the first zone anchor faster than the fastest rep.
  3. Floor = step outward one zone anchor at a time until the slowest rep fills at least 45% of the plot. Stop at 80% so a ragged session can't balloon.
  4. No zone table? Fall back to a span of 5× the rep spread on round gridlines. Same feel, no semantics.
  5. Hard floor on plausible pace — anything faster than the athlete's mile PR never touches the scale.
Why this beats copying them. Strava's tall bars are a fitting trick — the floor means nothing, so the height means nothing, it just feels better. Here the height is earned: it's distance below your own HMP pace. Same mass on the page, but the reason the bar is tall is the reason you're proud of it. That's the version worth shipping, and it's yours.
