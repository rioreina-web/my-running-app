What changed, and why it reads

The MILES and TLS lanes are now stacked by pace zone, colored on the one blue ramp from PaceSpectrum.swift. The two lanes tell the story together: miles are mostly pale — easy running is the foundation — while TLS weights the fast end, so the same interval Wednesday that is a short bar in MILES is a tall, navy-tipped bar in TLS. Where the deep blue lives is where the training stress came from.

The palette rule holds. Blue = pace, warm = mood, coral = alert. The old graphite bars existed because a blue bar "would read as a pace signal" — these bars now are pace signals, so the color is finally earning the rule rather than dodging it. Mood stays warm, the niggle dot stays coral, and no hue is shared.

Days without lap data stay graphite. A manual entry or an import with no splits (Fri Aug 14 here) draws the flat neutral bar and says so when tapped — we cannot say how it was distributed, and must not guess. Rest days stay empty. Three states, three looks.

Implementation notes

Data already exists per day on TrendsDay: zoneMiles for the MILES lane, zoneLoad (weighted minutes off the backend's continuous curve) for TLS. In TrendsMoodLanes.swift, drawBars grows a stacked variant that walks the day's zones slow→fast, bottom→top; the lane keeps one scale across the window (peak day = full height), exactly as today. Fallback: hasZoneBreakdown == false → today's flat bar. The zone→color map is the one already in TrendsReadView.zoneColor; recovery folds to the easy stop and the backend folds LT into hmp, so no new vocabulary. This prototype's TLS uses the discrete TrendsZoneWeight table — the app should keep preferring zoneLoad when present.
