One path, and why that's the smaller build

The first version of this split the questions into two rails — about this run and against the block — because only one analyzer in the registry accepts a workout id. That's an accurate description of the plumbing and a bad thing to show an athlete. She asked a question. The job is to answer it.

So: one rail, one path, one answer. Every question — tapped or typed — goes to the same endpoint with this session's context attached, and comes back as prose. It answers as well as it can with what it has, and says at the bottom what it read.

What got cut, and what that saves

CutWhy
The chart (series → AskChart)The session sheet is already three charts deep by the time she reaches the box. A fourth answers a question she didn't ask, and it's the most expensive thing on the response.
The fact gridFour tiles of numbers she just scrolled past. The prose can say the number.
The two-tier railTold her about our plumbing. See above.
"This analyzer isn't built"Never show an athlete a coverage gap she didn't ask about. Answer worse, don't refuse.
Chip → analyzer id bindingChips are now just prefilled questions. Same path as typing. One code path instead of two.

What got cut is rendering, not knowing. The context going into the answer gets richer, not thinner — see below.

What stays, because it's free

The one line at the bottom — "Read from this session's laps, your zones as of Aug 18, and the conditions at 6:29 AM" — is a string assembled from what the context builder actually loaded. It's not a computation and not a model output. It costs nothing and it's most of what made the old coverage row worth having.

It has to know the athlete, not just the run

A box that answers "was I holding back?" without knowing her volume, her phase, her goal and her history writes something fluent and useless. That failure is already on record in this repo — the old insight sent the chat agent a bare distance | duration | pace line and got back "I need more info — what's your weekly mileage?"

The block that fixes it already exists: loadAthleteStateBlock in generate-workout-insight. It reads seventeen columns of athlete_state in one query — volume, fitness as ranges rather than point times, declared races, training phase, the goal and the seconds-per-mile gap to it, load versus chronic, niggles surfaced but never diagnosed, behavioural patterns, recent training. It's complete and well-judged. It's just private to one function.

Phase B lifts it into _shared/context.ts and gives it to this box too. That's a move, not a design — which is why it's its own commit. Notice the "next one" answer above: the goal, the phase and the mileage are doing the work in the first paragraph. Without them it's just a paragraph about three splits.

One thing that comes with it: knowing the athlete better means niggle_recurrence sits in context on every turn, and a model handed the same flashcard forever raises it forever. That's diagnosed in ASK-V2-CONVERSATION.md §3, and the fix is a watermark in state rather than an instruction in the prompt. Ship without it; it's server-side if the knee starts turning up uninvited.

The fifteen, and why only five show

Full list and the gating in session-questions.ts. Every question is offered only when the session can answer it — "did I hold the pace across the reps?" on a steady six-miler produces a paragraph explaining there were no reps, which reads as the app not knowing what it's looking at. A shorter honest rail beats a longer one with two dead entries.

IntentQuestionsGated on
The readWhat's the read on this session?Always. Pinned first — the old row's job, and the safe default when she doesn't know what to ask.
ExecutionDid I run this the way it was written? · Did I hold the pace across the reps? · Did I fade in the back half? · Did I start too fast?A prescription, ≥2 reps, splits. Back half is withheld on easy runs — late drift is normal there, and offering the question invites the wrong answer.
EffortWas this actually easy? · How hard was this, really? · Was my heart rate where it should have been?Heart rate or splits. Actually easy only on easy and recovery runs — probably the most useful question in the set.
ConditionsWas that pace real, or was it the conditions? · How much did the hills cost me?Weather captured; elevation stream present.
ComparisonHow does this compare to the last one like it? · Could I have run this a month ago?A comparable prior exists; quality session only.
FitWas this the right session for where I am right now? · Does this get me closer to my goal pace?Always; goal + quality. These two lean hardest on athlete state — they're the questions that most reward getting §5.4 right.
BodyIs there anything here worth paying attention to?She mentioned something, or a body mention is open. Never offered unprompted on a clean session — inviting a worry question on a good run is its own kind of harm.
NextWhat should the next one look like?Quality or long runs.

The picker fills the four slots after the read one intent at a time before any intent gets a second. Without that pass, any session with splits fills the rail with four execution questions — each individually relevant, collectively a worse rail.

Where the agility actually lives

  • The questions come from the server. The list ships on the response, not in the binary. Changing what a long run asks versus a rep session is a config edit and a deploy — not an App Store release and a week of review. This is the single most valuable decision here and it costs one field. The instinct to "just hardcode the five" removes the only cheap lever this surface has.
  • Every question is logged. analysis_queries already records them. Ship prose-first, read a month of what people actually type, then build computation for the top five. That's a far better way to choose the next analyzer than guessing from a registry doc.
  • The computed layer is additive later. If a question turns out to be worth grounding in real math, that analyzer's facts get handed to the same prose call as context. The UI never changes — the answer just gets more accurate. Nothing built now has to be torn out.

The one trade-off, stated plainly

Prose answers are not number-guarded. Layer-2 narration over analyzer facts can't speak a number that isn't in facts; a conversational answer can. That guard isn't being removed — it's just not on this path.

Worth being clear that this is not a new exposure: the insight paragraph this box replaces is already pure prose over a session context, generated for every voice memo, with no number guard at all. The box gives the athlete the choice of question and stops generating unasked. On the numbers, it's the same risk it already ships with.
