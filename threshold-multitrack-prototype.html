What the columns say

Rest collapsed while pace held.

The clearest signal is the bottom lane. Your recoveries went from 228s (27 Mar) and 234s (7 Apr) down to 61s, 55s and 77s across July — while rep pace stayed at 5:15–5:45/mi and rep HR sat at 164–166. Same work, a third of the recovery. That is the cleanest fitness gain in this whole dataset and none of your current surfaces show it.

Read that against the HR lane directly above: HR in band is flat at 165–166 bpm through July, versus 169 in late March. Holding threshold pace at lower HR and shorter rest, in worse heat, is the definition of a block working.

Threshold density is a barbell, not a line.

Density ran 10–12% through late March and April, collapsed to 0.9–2.5% across mid-May to mid-July, then jumped back to 7.2% and 10.6% in the last two weeks. Six weeks near zero is a long time to go without work in the band — and four of those weeks show 0.0% because the laps never landed inside it, not because you didn't run hard.

The heat lane explains part of it. Dew point sat at 70–74°F through that stretch with a 8–14 s/mi pace penalty, which is enough to push a genuine threshold effort out of a pace-defined band entirely. That is the argument for evaluating band membership on heat-adjusted pace, not raw pace — the adjusted number is already in running_workout_laps.

A pace band that a runner falls out of every July isn't measuring threshold work. It's measuring weather.

The band moved under you.

HMP drifted from 5:20 to 5:35 and back to 5:23 across the window, tracking fitness_snapshots. Worth showing, because a run judged in-band in April (band 5:06–5:38) would sit outside today's tighter 5:07–5:39. Two snapshots in the series are junk — 6:01 and 6:25 half-marathon paces on days when the real estimate was 5:20 — and are excluded here. Worth a sanity filter in the pipeline.

Notes for building it

Band membership should use heat-adjusted pace. Six weeks of 0–2% density in peak summer is mostly the correction not being applied.

Session density needs an outlier rule. Four sessions here include one long break (a stoplight, a bathroom stop, a 1,760s pause on 24 Jul) that drags the mean up. Use the median rest, or trim rests above 3× the median — those bars are drawn pale.

Lane 1 is the one to ship first. The band plus the laps is legible on its own and answers "am I doing threshold work" in one glance. The other six lanes are the drill-down.

Mile auto-laps pollute the rep detector. Long runs with 2 rest laps look like 24-rep sessions. Gate session density on rep count between 4 and 16 and mean rep distance under 2,000m.
