One band at LT, one at your target MP

Both anchors move, but they don’t mean the same thing. LT is measured — the app derives it from your testing, so moving it is an override: you’re saying the derivation is wrong. MP is a goal — there is nothing to derive, only a race you’ve decided to run. Same control, opposite direction of authority. Each band also gets its own width.

Move the target and watch two things change at once: whether the bands stay clear of each other, and how many of your MP sessions still count as MP work at all.

Two anchors, two different controls

LT is an override. The app derives it and should keep deriving it; the control exists because a derivation from a bad test, a hot week, or a stale field test is worse than a number the athlete knows. Ship it as a correction with the derived value still visible — “derived 5:20, overridden to 5:17” — so it’s never silent, and let it decay back to derived when a fresh test lands.

MP is an input. There is no measurement to override: two athletes with identical thresholds can be racing a 2:26 and a 2:37. This should read straight off TrendsBlockGoal — race date and distance already live there, a target pace or goal time is the missing field — with a manual set for anyone who hasn’t entered a race.

The practical difference is what happens when they’re wrong. A wrong LT anchor makes the section mis-grade work the athlete genuinely did. A wrong MP target makes it grade that work against the wrong race — the sessions were real either way.

Moving the target has two consequences, not one

1 · It moves the band toward or away from LT. A faster goal pushes MP up into threshold territory and the two bands start overlapping again — at LT ±3% and MP ±3%, a 5:35 target reintroduces a five-second double-count. Raising the LT anchor does the same thing from the other side. Clearance is a property of both anchors and both widths, so it has to be checked whenever any of the four changes, not set once at install.

2 · It changes what counts as MP work. Sessions that sat comfortably in the band at a 5:47 target fall out of it at 5:35, because you weren’t running that pace. This is correct — a 5:50 segment is not marathon-pace work for a 2:26 marathoner — but it means the section can go from six sessions to zero on a single control change, and the design has to survive that. Every layout here has an empty state for exactly this reason.

Separate widths, and why

The two bands don’t deserve the same skirt. A twenty-minute tempo holds a tight pace; forty minutes of marathon pace inside a long run drifts more, especially late. A narrow LT band with a slightly wider MP band is the shape that matches how the work actually gets run — the controls above let you find it rather than assuming ±5% for both. The constraint is only that the edges must not cross.

What the mockup can’t show you

It filters whole sessions by their average band pace. The app filters laps, then applies minTotalMinutes and minBlockMinutes. So real behaviour is softer at the edges — a session near the boundary loses some minutes rather than all of them — but stricter overall, because a session can lose enough minutes to fail the gate entirely. Expect real numbers to fall further than these do.
